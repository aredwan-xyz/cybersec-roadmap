# Lesson 1: What cybersecurity is

> **In one sentence:** Cybersecurity is the work of keeping information and the systems that hold it secret, correct and working, even when someone is trying to break them.

**Time:** about 15 minutes · **You need:** a browser

## The big idea

Think about your home. You lock the door so strangers cannot read your letters. You would want to know if someone changed the name on your post box. And you want the lights to work when you need them.

Computers need the same three kinds of care, called the **CIA triad** (nothing to do with spies):

- **Confidentiality**: keep it secret. Only the right people can see your messages or bank details.
- **Integrity**: keep it correct. Nobody can quietly change your data, such as the amount on a payment or the grade on a report.
- **Availability**: keep it working. The website or hospital system is there when people need it.

Almost every security problem breaks at least one of these. A leaked password breaks confidentiality; a faked invoice, integrity; a website knocked offline, availability.

**Cybersecurity** is everything people do to protect those three things: your habits and settings, the software companies build, and the teams who watch for trouble. The simple steps in the next lessons stop most attacks ordinary people face.

## How it works

Security people use three words that sound alike but mean different things:

| Word | Plain meaning | Home example |
|---|---|---|
| **Threat** | Someone or something that could cause harm | A burglar in the area |
| **Vulnerability** | A weakness that harm could get through | A window that does not lock |
| **Risk** | How likely harm is, and how bad it would be | A burglar finding that window while you are away |

You usually cannot remove threats. What you can do is fix vulnerabilities (lock the window) and lower risk (keep valuables out of sight). Good security means spending effort where the risk is highest.

**Who are the attackers, and why?** Most are not hooded geniuses targeting you personally. The common types are:

- **Criminals** who want money. They send scam messages to millions, steal accounts to resell, or lock files and demand payment. This is by far the largest group.
- **Insiders**: staff who misuse access they already have, on purpose or by accident.
- **Hacktivists** who want attention for a cause.
- **Governments** who spy on rivals, companies or critics.

Most attacks are automated. You are rarely chosen; you are one of many doors being tried, and a locked door usually makes attackers move on.

## In real life

In May 2017 malicious software called WannaCry spread worldwide. It locked files on infected Windows computers and demanded payment to unlock them. Hospitals in England's National Health Service were badly hit, and some appointments and operations were cancelled. That is an attack on availability.

The weakness it used was already known. Microsoft had released a fix in March 2017, two months earlier, but many affected computers had not installed it. A known vulnerability, left unfixed, turned a threat into real harm.

## Try it

1. Pick one account that matters to you, such as your email. Write down one threat to it, one vulnerability it might have, and what would happen if it were broken into. That is a tiny risk assessment.
2. For each bad outcome, note which part of the CIA triad it breaks.
3. Enter your own email address at [Have I Been Pwned](https://haveibeenpwned.com) to see if it appeared in a known data breach. If it did, Lesson 2 shows you what to do.

## Key words

- **Cybersecurity**: protecting computers, networks and data from harm, theft and damage.
- **CIA triad**: confidentiality, integrity and availability, the three things security protects.
- **Threat**: a person or event that could cause harm.
- **Vulnerability**: a weakness that could let harm happen.
- **Risk**: the chance of harm combined with how bad it would be.
- **Data breach**: when private information is exposed to people who should not have it.

## Check yourself

**1. A hacker changes the delivery address on your online order without you noticing. Which part of the CIA triad is broken?**

- A. Confidentiality
- B. Integrity
- C. Availability

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Integrity means data stays correct. The address was changed without permission.

</details>

**2. Your phone has an update waiting that fixes a known security hole. What is the security hole?**

- A. A threat
- B. A risk
- C. A vulnerability

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** A vulnerability is a weakness. The threat is whoever might use it.

</details>

**3. Why do most attackers send scam messages to huge numbers of people?**

- A. Because most attacks are automated and they only need a few people to fall for it
- B. Because they have studied each person carefully first
- C. Because sending to many people is required by law

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** A small success rate across millions of cheap, automated messages still pays.

</details>
