# Lesson 14: Jobs in cybersecurity and how to start

> **In one sentence:** Cybersecurity has many different jobs, most people enter through a general IT or software role first, and showing what you can do matters more than collecting certificates.

**Time:** about 15 minutes · **You need:** a browser

## The big idea

Think of a hospital. It is not staffed only by surgeons. There are nurses, lab technicians, pharmacists, radiographers and people who write the safety rules. Each role needs different skills, and very few people start as surgeons.

Cybersecurity is the same. "Working in security" can mean watching alerts at night, breaking into apps with permission, building safe systems, or making sure a company follows the law. Some roles are very technical. Some are mostly about policy and writing.

Be honest about the door in. Employers usually want people who already understand the computers, networks and software they will protect. That is why most people arrive from another tech job: **IT support** (also called **helpdesk**, the people who fix laptops and reset passwords), system administration, or software development. A year or two there is not a detour. It is where you learn what you will later protect.

The good news: free labs and courses are open to anyone, and what you build with them becomes your evidence.

## How it works

The main roles you will see in job ads:

- **SOC analyst:** works in a **SOC (security operations center)**, a team that watches alerts, decides which are real attacks, and starts the response. A common first security job.
- **Penetration tester:** attacks systems with permission and writes reports on what to fix. Junior openings are few and competitive.
- **Security engineer:** builds and runs the defences, such as logging and access controls. One of the largest groups of jobs.
- **Cloud security engineer:** does the same for systems in AWS, Azure or Google Cloud, with a heavy focus on identity and access.
- **GRC analyst:** GRC means **governance, risk and compliance**: writing security policies, assessing risk, and checking a company meets laws and standards.
- **AppSec engineer:** **application security** specialists help developers find and fix flaws in their software. Suits people who can program.
- **AI security engineer:** protects AI products against problems like prompt injection. A young field where published work counts for a lot.

Two honest points about getting hired:

1. **Proof of skill beats certificates alone.** A **certificate** (such as CompTIA Security+) shows you passed an exam, and some employers filter on them. But hiring managers want to see you can do the work: write-ups of labs you finished, a small tool you built, or a documented home lab. One or two certificates plus real work beats a long list of certificates with nothing to show.
2. **Community helps.** Local **BSides** conferences, OWASP chapters and CTF teams are where people find mentors and job leads.

## In real life

A typical path: someone with no tech background takes a helpdesk job. They reset passwords, fix laptops and learn how the company's accounts and network fit together. In the evenings they study for a beginner security certificate and complete free labs, writing a short public note about each. Later they ask to help the security team with small tasks, like reviewing who has admin access. When a junior security role opens, they apply with real experience, a certificate and write-ups that prove what they can do.

## Try it

1. Search a job site for "SOC analyst", "GRC analyst" and "cloud security engineer" in your area. Open one ad for each and list the three skills that appear most often.
2. Pick one free beginner path, such as TryHackMe's "Pre Security", and complete the first room. Write three sentences about what you learned. That is your first piece of evidence.

## Key words

- **IT support (helpdesk)**: the team that fixes everyday tech problems for staff; a common entry route.
- **SOC (security operations center)**: a team that monitors alerts and responds to attacks.
- **Security engineer**: someone who builds and runs an organization's defences.
- **GRC (governance, risk and compliance)**: security work focused on policy, risk and following laws and standards.
- **AppSec (application security)**: finding and fixing security flaws in software.
- **Certificate**: proof that you passed a specific exam, such as CompTIA Security+.

## Check yourself

**1. How do most people get their first cybersecurity job?**

- A. Straight from a single online course
- B. After experience in IT support, system administration or software development
- C. By winning a hacking competition

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Employers want people who understand the systems they protect, and general IT or development jobs teach exactly that.

</details>

**2. Which role focuses mostly on policy, risk and meeting laws and standards?**

- A. Penetration tester
- B. AppSec engineer
- C. GRC analyst

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** GRC stands for governance, risk and compliance. It involves more writing and judgement than coding.

</details>

**3. Which is the strongest evidence for a hiring manager?**

- A. Public write-ups of labs you completed, plus one relevant certificate
- B. A list of ten certificates with no projects
- C. Saying you are passionate about security

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Certificates can help you pass filters, but visible work shows you can actually do the job.

</details>
