# Glossary

Plain-English meanings for the words you will meet in these lessons and across cybersecurity.

## A

- **Access control**: the rules that decide who can see or change something.
- **Account takeover**: when an attacker gains control of someone else's online account.
- **Adware**: unwanted software that floods you with ads, sometimes while tracking you.
- **AI agent**: an AI model that can take actions, such as browsing the web, sending messages or running code.
- **Antivirus**: software that looks for and removes known malicious programs.
- **AppSec (application security)**: the work of finding and fixing security flaws in software.
- **Attack surface**: all the places where an attacker could try to get into a system.
- **Authentication**: proving who you are, for example with a password or fingerprint.
- **Authorization**: deciding what someone is allowed to do after they have proved who they are.

## B

- **Backdoor**: a hidden way into a system that skips normal security checks.
- **Backup**: a separate copy of your data that you can restore if the original is lost or locked.
- **Blue team**: the defenders who detect and respond to attacks.
- **Botnet**: a large group of infected devices controlled together by an attacker.
- **Breach**: an incident where data is accessed or taken by someone who should not have it.
- **Brute force**: guessing a password by trying huge numbers of possibilities.
- **Bucket**: a container for files in cloud storage, which can be private or public.
- **Bug bounty**: a program that pays people for reporting security flaws within a published scope.

## C

- **Certificate (exam)**: proof that you passed a specific exam, such as CompTIA Security+.
- **CIA triad (confidentiality, integrity, availability)**: the three goals of security: keep data secret, correct and accessible.
- **Cloud**: computing power and storage you rent from a provider and reach over the internet.
- **Cookie**: a small file a website stores in your browser to remember you, for example that you are logged in.
- **Credential stuffing**: trying usernames and passwords leaked from one site on many other sites.
- **CTF (capture the flag)**: a legal security game where you solve challenges to find hidden text.
- **CVE (Common Vulnerabilities and Exposures)**: a public ID number given to a known security flaw, like CVE-2021-44228.

## D

- **Dark web**: hidden websites reachable only with special software, where stolen data is often traded.
- **Data exfiltration**: secretly moving data out of a system to an attacker.
- **DDoS (distributed denial of service)**: flooding a service with traffic from many machines so real users cannot reach it.
- **Deepfake**: fake audio, video or images made with AI to look like a real person.
- **DNS (Domain Name System)**: the internet's address book, which turns names like example.com into IP addresses.

## E

- **EDR (endpoint detection and response)**: software on computers that watches for suspicious behaviour and helps stop attacks.
- **Encryption**: scrambling data so only someone with the right key can read it.
- **End-to-end encryption**: encryption where only the sender and receiver can read messages, not the service in between.
- **Endpoint**: any device that connects to a network, such as a laptop, phone or server.
- **Ethical hacker**: someone who attacks systems only with the owner's permission, to help fix weaknesses.
- **Exploit**: code or a method that takes advantage of a vulnerability.

## F

- **Firewall**: a filter that allows or blocks network traffic based on rules.
- **Forensics**: collecting and examining digital evidence to understand what happened.

## G

- **GRC (governance, risk and compliance)**: security work focused on policy, risk and following laws and standards.

## H

- **Hacker**: someone who makes a system do something its builders did not intend; not always a criminal.
- **Hashing**: turning data into a fixed-length fingerprint that cannot be turned back into the original.
- **HTTPS (Hypertext Transfer Protocol Secure)**: the encrypted version of the protocol web browsers use, shown by a padlock.

## I

- **IAM (identity and access management)**: the system that controls who can do what in an organization or cloud account.
- **IDS (intrusion detection system)**: a tool that watches network or computer activity and alerts on signs of attack.
- **Incident response**: the planned steps a team takes to contain and recover from an attack.
- **IP address (Internet Protocol address)**: a number that identifies a device on a network so data can reach it.

## K

- **Keylogger**: malware that secretly records what you type.

## L

- **Least privilege**: giving each person or program only the access it truly needs.
- **Lethal trifecta**: private data, untrusted content and a way to send data out, all in one AI system.
- **LLM (large language model)**: a program trained on vast amounts of text to predict and generate language.
- **Log**: a record of events on a system, such as logins and errors.

## M

- **Malware**: any software designed to harm, spy on or take control of a device.
- **MFA (multi-factor authentication)**: logging in with a second proof beyond your password, such as an app prompt or security key.
- **Misconfiguration**: a wrong or careless setting that leaves a system exposed.
- **MITRE ATT&CK**: a public catalogue of the techniques real attackers use.

## N

- **Network**: computers and devices connected so they can share data.
- **NIST (National Institute of Standards and Technology)**: a US agency that publishes widely used security guidance.

## O

- **OSINT (open-source intelligence)**: information gathered from public sources such as websites and social media.
- **OWASP (Open Worldwide Application Security Project)**: a nonprofit community that publishes free guidance on software security, including the OWASP Top 10.

## P

- **Passkey**: a password replacement stored on your device that signs you in with your fingerprint, face or PIN and cannot be phished.
- **Password manager**: an app that creates, stores and fills in strong, unique passwords for you.
- **Patch**: an update that fixes a bug or security flaw.
- **Payload**: the part of an attack that does the actual harm once it gets in.
- **Pentest (penetration test)**: a paid, time-limited attack on agreed systems, ending in a written report.
- **Phishing**: tricking people with fake messages into clicking links, opening files or giving away information.
- **Port**: a numbered doorway on a device that a specific network service listens on.
- **Privilege escalation**: gaining more access than you were given, such as going from normal user to admin.
- **Prompt**: the text given to an AI model, including hidden instructions and your message.
- **Prompt injection**: hiding instructions in text so an AI follows the attacker instead of its owner.

## R

- **Ransomware**: malware that locks or steals your files and demands payment.
- **Red team**: a group that plays a realistic attacker to test an organization's defences.
- **Risk**: the chance that a threat will exploit a weakness, combined with how bad the harm would be.
- **Rootkit**: malware that hides deep in a system to avoid being found.

## S

- **Sandbox**: an isolated space where code can run without affecting the rest of the system.
- **Scope**: the written list of what you are allowed to test, when and how.
- **Secret**: a password, key or token that lets a program prove who it is.
- **Shared responsibility model**: the split between what a cloud provider secures and what the customer secures.
- **SIEM (security information and event management)**: a system that collects logs from many sources and raises alerts.
- **Smishing**: phishing by text message.
- **SOC (security operations center)**: a team that monitors alerts and responds to attacks.
- **Social engineering**: manipulating people, rather than computers, into breaking security.
- **Spoofing**: faking where something comes from, such as an email sender or a phone number.
- **Spyware**: malware that secretly watches what you do and reports it.
- **SQL injection**: an attack that slips database commands into a form or web address to read or change data.
- **Supply chain attack**: attacking a target through software or a supplier it trusts.
- **System prompt**: the developer's hidden instructions that set how an AI app behaves.

## T

- **Threat**: anyone or anything that could cause harm, such as a criminal group or a flood.
- **Threat actor**: a person or group that carries out attacks, from lone criminals to government teams.
- **Threat model**: a structured look at what you are protecting, from whom, and how it could go wrong.
- **Token**: a digital pass that proves you are logged in or allowed to use a service.
- **Trojan**: malware disguised as a useful or harmless program.
- **Two-factor authentication (2FA)**: MFA that uses exactly two proofs, usually a password and a code.

## U

- **Untrusted content**: any data from outside your control, such as web pages, emails or uploaded files.

## V

- **Vishing**: phishing by phone call.
- **VPN (virtual private network)**: an encrypted tunnel for your internet traffic between your device and a VPN server.
- **Vulnerability**: a weakness that an attacker could use.

## W

- **WAF (web application firewall)**: a filter that blocks common attacks against websites.
- **Worm**: malware that spreads by itself from one computer to another.

## X

- **XSS (cross-site scripting)**: an attack that sneaks malicious script into a web page so it runs in other visitors' browsers.

## Z

- **Zero trust**: a security approach that never trusts a user or device automatically and checks every request.
- **Zero-day**: a flaw that attackers know about before the maker has released a fix.
