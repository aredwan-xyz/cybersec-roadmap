# Market focus (researched 2026-11-22)

Figures below come from web search summaries of industry surveys and news coverage, not from the primary reports. Treat the percentages as directional.

### What the market is saying

- **AI and cloud are the two most wanted technical skills.** ISC2's 2025 workforce study had AI as the top skill need (about 41–44% of respondents) and cloud security second (about 36–40%). Its 2026 follow-ups say the same.
- **The shortage is of skills, not bodies.** ISC2 reports skills gaps now outweigh headcount gaps. Employers want fewer, more capable people.
- **Entry-level is the worst place to compete.** Coverage from August to October 2026 reports that security job ads requiring AI skills doubled in a year while junior hiring stalled and overall hiring hit a multi-year low. Gartner expects AI to take over about half of Tier 1 SOC analyst work by 2028. In one Canadian dataset, only about 5% of security postings were junior.
- **New roles are being created around AI.** A SANS-linked survey reports most organizations creating AI security roles: AI security engineer, AI/ML security specialist, AI governance analyst.
- **Security engineering is the largest category of postings.** Security engineer and cloud security engineer roles outnumber pure analyst roles in G7 job-ad data.
- **OT and IoT security talent is scarce.** Sources agree on the shortage and that smart buildings need a rare blend of IT, OT and building-systems knowledge. Postings are fewer and usually ask for experience, and I found no solid current job-count data.
- **Regulation is adding steady demand** for governance and compliance skills (NIS2, DORA, CMMC, SEC rules, and AI regulation).
- **Canada:** Job Bank rates cybersecurity analysts at moderate risk of labour shortage for 2024–2033. Demand is concentrated at mid and senior level.

### Recommendation

**Do not aim for a generic entry-level security job.** That segment is shrinking and automating. Add security to the engineering experience you already have, so you compete as an experienced engineer with a security specialty.

Focus, in this order:

1. **AI security (primary).** The fastest-growing need with the fewest qualified people, and builders of AI products have a head start. Learn prompt injection and agent abuse, securing tool-using agents and MCP-style integrations, model and data supply chain, the OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications, MITRE ATLAS, and the NIST AI Risk Management Framework.
2. **Application and cloud security (the base it rests on).** AI security is mostly AppSec and cloud security applied to a new kind of system. Learn web and API vulnerabilities (PortSwigger Academy), identity and access management in one cloud (AWS or Azure), and CI/CD and secrets hygiene.
3. **OT/IoT and smart-building security (the differentiator).** A smaller market, but with little competition and a direct fit with existing smart-building work. Learn BACnet and Modbus weaknesses, network segmentation for building systems, IEC 62443, and NIST SP 800-82.
4. **Security automation with AI agents (a skill, not a track).** Employers report hands-on experience with AI agents as the hardest thing to find. Build detections, triage and reporting workflows with agents.

Deprioritize for now: Tier 1 SOC as a career plan, pure penetration testing as a first job, and collecting certificates beyond Security+ and one cloud cert.

### Limits of this recommendation

- **It is not validated against real postings.** It rests on skills surveys, not on a count of actual AI security or OT security roles, their seniority, or how many are open to remote or Canadian applicants. Read 20 postings before committing (see "Career mechanics").
- **Clearance and screening requirements are not accounted for.** They may rule out part of the local market.
- **The "builders have a head start" claim is unproven.** Hiring managers may still want security experience first.
- **The OT/smart-building niche has the weakest evidence:** agreement that talent is scarce, and no posting counts.
- **An alternative was not weighed:** staying in your current line of work and adding a security section to existing smart-building and AI offers, without a career pivot. The 90-day plan's exit criteria point back to this option.
